# Conclusion

## Access to Essential Services Depends on Transport Choice

This study investigated spatial variations in car dependency and access to essential services across **56 Statistical Areas Level 1 (SA1s) in Burnie, Tasmania**, comparing private-car and public bus accessibility within a **30-minute travel-time threshold**.

The analysis considered six service categories: **schools, healthcare facilities, pharmacies, supermarkets, greenspaces and leisure facilities**.

The findings reveal a widespread **accessibility advantage for private cars**, particularly beyond the northern urban areas where essential services and public transport connections are more concentrated.

These patterns demonstrate how transport availability and the spatial distribution of services influence access to everyday opportunities (Geurs & van Wee, 2004; Levinson & Wu, 2020).

## Key Findings

| Main finding | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Private cars provide greater accessibility** | Residents without access to a private vehicle may face greater difficulty reaching essential services under the modelled conditions. |
| **Public transport accessibility varies spatially** | Parts of northern urban Burnie demonstrate smaller accessibility differences between private cars and public buses. |
| **High CDI does not always mean good accessibility** | Some SA1s have high car dependency despite limited accessibility even by private car. CDI must be interpreted alongside both transport-mode accessibility scores. |
| **Socio-economic disadvantage adds important context** | The CDI–IRSD relationship indicates that car dependency does not consistently coincide with greater socio-economic disadvantage. Both measures are needed to investigate potential inequalities. |

The findings highlight the importance of evaluating accessibility through **reachable opportunities rather than transport availability alone**, as emphasised by Geurs and van Wee (2004).

## Implications for Burnie

The results suggest that improving accessibility in Burnie requires consideration of both transport connections and the distribution of essential services.

Three potential planning priorities emerge:

1. **Strengthen public transport connections:** Investigate bus routes, service frequency and connections between residential areas and essential destinations.

2. **Address areas with limited accessibility by both modes:** Examine whether service distribution, network connectivity or geographic constraints contribute to reduced access.

3. **Consider socio-economic disadvantage:** Use CDI alongside ABS SEIFA IRSD data to identify areas where limited transport alternatives may overlap with greater socio-economic disadvantage (ABS, 2023).

These priorities provide directions for further investigation rather than definitive recommendations for specific routes or infrastructure projects.

## Where to Focus Next

### International Example: Connecting Ireland

Ireland's **Connecting Ireland Rural Mobility Plan** provides an example of how regional public transport connectivity can be strengthened through expanded routes and improved services.

According to the National Transport Authority's *Connecting Ireland Phase 2 Review*, Phase 2 connected **190 additional towns and villages**, including improved connections to hospitals and colleges.

Although Ireland and Burnie differ in population distribution, geography and transport systems, this initiative illustrates an approach that could inform future investigations into improving regional accessibility without relying exclusively on private vehicles (National Transport Authority, *Connecting Ireland Phase 2 Review*).

## Final Remarks

This study demonstrates that **car dependency is not simply a measure of car accessibility**, but reflects differences in the opportunities reachable by private car and public transport.

By combining spatial accessibility modelling, the Car Dependency Index and socio-economic indicators, the analysis provides a more comprehensive understanding of transport accessibility across Burnie.

While private cars generally offer greater access to essential services, the findings also reveal spatial differences that cannot be explained by car dependency alone.

Ultimately, improving accessibility in regional cities requires attention to **transport alternatives, service distribution and socio-economic equity**. These considerations provide a foundation for further investigation and more inclusive transport planning in Burnie.
