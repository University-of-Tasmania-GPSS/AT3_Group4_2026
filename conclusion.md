# Conclusion

## Access to Essential Services Depends on Transport Choice

Across Burnie's **56 SA1s**, the 30-minute accessibility map shows a widespread **advantage for private cars** over public buses. Lower car dependency occurs in parts of northern urban Burnie, where the mapped examples show stronger bus accessibility.

The comparison covers **schools, healthcare facilities, pharmacies, supermarkets, greenspaces and leisure facilities**.

| Main finding | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Car advantage is widespread** | People unable to use a car may have fewer reachable services under the modelled conditions |
| **Higher bus accessibility narrows the gap** | Stronger bus connections can support access without a private vehicle |
| **High CDI can coexist with poor car access** | CDI must be interpreted alongside both accessibility scores |
| **Equity requires IRSD evidence** | High dependency and low IRSD should be examined together before identifying priority areas |

## Where to Focus Next

### International Example: Connecting Ireland

Ireland expanded rural bus connections through new routes and improved services. Phase 2 connected **190 additional towns and villages**, including new connections to hospitals and colleges. This offers an approach Burnie could investigate to improve access without a car.
